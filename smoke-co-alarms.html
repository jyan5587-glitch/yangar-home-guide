Smoke and CO Alarms Before Holiday Guests

November is a good time to test every alarm before family visits, holiday cooking, and colder nights with heaters running.

Test and replace

  • Press the test button on every smoke and carbon monoxide (CO) alarm once a month, and again before guests arrive.
  • Replace batteries at least once a year on battery-powered units, or right away if you hear chirping.
  • Many sealed 10-year units have a built-in battery; replace the whole alarm when it reaches the end-of-life date on the label, not just the battery.
  • Replace any alarm that fails the test, is older than the manufacturer's life span (often about 10 years), or has yellowed or damaged housing.

Where to place (general consumer guidance)

  • Smoke alarms on every level, outside sleeping areas, and inside each bedroom when practical.
  • CO alarms near sleeping areas and on each level that has fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage.
  • Follow the manufacturer's height and spacing notes; keep alarms away from drafts, bathrooms, and cooking steam that cause nuisance trips.
  • This is general consumer guidance, not a code inspection. Local rules and your insurance carrier may set more specific requirements.

What stays DIY vs. licensed work

  • Battery swaps, monthly tests, and swapping plug-in or battery-only units are usually DIY.
  • New hardwired installs, rewiring, or interconnecting alarms on the house electrical system need a TDLR-licensed electrician.
  • Landlords and property managers: document the date of each test and battery or unit replacement; keep a simple log for your records and for tenants.
  • If an alarm keeps sounding with no clear cause, ventilate, leave if anyone feels ill, and call emergency services for a suspected CO event. Have fuel appliances checked by a qualified pro.
